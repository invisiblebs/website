Sources & accounts

Source = a public register or directory we read (a licence list, L2Beat, a grants list). Account = a company found in a source, with its facts. Accounts become Leads in the next tab once a decision-maker role is attached and scored. Providers stay blocked until approved.

Accounts

Signals: people found by what they do, not by their title

A lens is a behaviour that reveals the problem we solve: someone writing publicly about post-quantum migration or prover soundness, a DAO voting an audit budget, a protocol that just lost funds to a forged proof, a company advertising a cryptography role, an org shipping PQ code. Each row is one observed fact with its evidence link. A signal that matches an account raises that account's score; a signal from a person we cannot place becomes its own lead whose only channel is a public reply in the thread they started, which is lawful everywhere.

Leads

Lead = one decision-maker role at an account (CTO, CISO, Head of Protocol…) that scores 60 or more on the ICP model and has at least one lawful channel in its country. Until a contact-data provider is approved the person is a placeholder shown as [pending]; the company, role, score and channel are real. Click a row to see why it scored, the law for its country, and its messages.

Messages

Every message rendered in the latest run, exactly as it would go out, best-scored lead first. Edit the text in place (it is re-checked by the claims linter and any earlier approval is cleared), then approve or reject. Only approved, lint-clean messages can be sent from a pilot.

Pilot

A pilot is a batch of up to 50 messages you review one by one before anything is sent. Email batches are written to an outbox on disk. LinkedIn batches are never sent by software: the console prepares the note and the DM, you send them by hand from your own account, and log it here.

How the batch is chosen

Building only writes a file; it does not send.

Follow-ups due

Touch 2 goes four days after touch 1, touch 3 five days after touch 2, and only while nobody has replied, bounced or opted out. Marking a message as sent by hand starts the clock.

Outcomes

What actually happened, per lead, against what the simulation predicted. Log every step from the lead drawer or the pilot rows; unsubscribes and bounces go straight to the suppression list.
